# Codeine cough-syrup withdrawal: cited research summary (v2)
Compiled 4 Nov 2026 IST for NO LEAN's Withdrawal guide. General information, not medical advice. Per the owner's direction the app lists no helpline numbers; red flags point to SOS / trusted contact / doctor / nearest hospital.

## 1. What is in the syrup and why timing differs
- Typical Indian codeine cough syrups contain **codeine phosphate ~10 mg per 5 ml** plus **chlorpheniramine maleate (~4 mg/5 ml)** or, in some brands, **promethazine** or triprolidine. [Mattoo et al. 1997; Indian J Med Sci 1999]
- **Codeine half-life ≈ 3 h** (converted to morphine by CYP2D6, ~2–3 h). Short half-life → withdrawal starts early (≈ 8–24 h) and the acute phase is short (≈ 5–10 days). [StatPearls: Codeine; StatPearls: Opioid Withdrawal]
- **Chlorpheniramine half-life ≈ 20 h (12–43 h); promethazine ≈ 10–19 h.** Stopping long-term high-dose first-generation antihistamines can cause **rebound insomnia, restlessness, anxiety, nausea, sweating and runny nose ("cholinergic rebound")** that can overlap or appear slightly later (days 1–4). [MedlinePlus: Chlorpheniramine; StatPearls: Promethazine; Lader on antihistamine rebound]
- People often take very large volumes (≈ 100–600 ml/day, i.e. ~200–1200 mg codeine/day) for years. [Mattoo 1997; Indian J Med Sci 1999; Magnitude of Substance Use in India 2019 (AIIMS/MoSJE)]

## 2. Hour-by-hour (short-acting opioid such as codeine)
| Time since last dose | What's common |
|---|---|
| 0–6 h | Usually still comfortable; first cravings and anxiety. |
| 6–12 h | Yawning, watery eyes, runny nose, sweating, restlessness, anxiety. |
| 12–24 h | Aches, goosebumps, dilated pupils, irritability, poor sleep, hot/cold flushes. |
| 24–48 h | Peak begins: stomach cramps, diarrhoea, nausea/vomiting, strong cravings, insomnia, raised pulse/BP. |
| 48–72 h | Peak continues; most people feel worst here. Dehydration risk. |
| Day 3–5 | Physical symptoms start lifting; fatigue, low appetite, poor sleep, cravings. |
| Day 5–7 | Most body symptoms gone for typical doses; sleep and mood still off. |
| Day 7–14 | Heavy/long-term users may still have aches, gut upset, insomnia; residual antihistamine rebound sleep issues. |
| Weeks 2–4+ | PAWS: low mood, anxiety, low energy, poor sleep, cravings in waves. |
| Months 1–6 (sometimes longer) | Gradual improvement; protracted abstinence syndrome episodes triggered by stress/cues. |
Sources: MedlinePlus; StatPearls Opioid Withdrawal; WHO 2009; SAMHSA TIP 63; SAMHSA Advisory "Protracted Withdrawal" (2010).

## 3. What changes severity and duration
- **Higher daily dose** and **longer duration of use** → stronger and longer acute symptoms. [StatPearls; WHO 2009]
- **Stop-start (relapse) patterns:** a short relapse after a few days restarts the clock but usually produces milder withdrawal than the first. Tolerance drops, so a relapse at the old dose risks overdose. [MedlinePlus]
- **Tapering vs stopping at once:** supervised tapering (or buprenorphine/methadone-assisted withdrawal) reduces symptom severity and drop-out; abrupt self-withdrawal ("cold turkey") is more intense but rarely dangerous in healthy adults. NICE recommends buprenorphine or methadone as first-line for detox, lofexidine as an alternative; tapers over up to 4 weeks (community, up to 12). [NICE CG52; WHO 2009; SAMHSA TIP 63]
- Health conditions (heart disease, pregnancy, epilepsy, dehydration, mental health) need medical supervision.

## 4. COWS (Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale)
11 items: resting pulse, sweating, restlessness, pupil size, bone/joint aches, runny nose/tearing, GI upset, tremor, yawning, anxiety/irritability, gooseflesh. Scores: 5–12 mild, 13–24 moderate, 25–36 moderately severe, >36 severe. Clinician-rated; the app's self check-in is inspired by its items but is not a COWS score. [Wesson & Ling 2003]

## 5. Do
- Fluids/ORS small and often; small bland meals (banana, rice, curd, khichdi, toast); rest; warm baths; light walks; sleep routine; tell a trusted person; see a doctor early (supervised withdrawal is easier and safer).

## 6. OTC symptom relief (adults; check with a pharmacist)
- **ORS** — for diarrhoea/vomiting, sip after each loose stool.
- **Paracetamol** 500 mg–1 g every 4–6 h, max 4 g/24 h (lower with liver disease, low weight, regular alcohol). [NHS]
- **Ibuprofen** 200–400 mg every 6–8 h with food, max 1,200 mg/24 h OTC; avoid with ulcers, kidney disease, heart failure, some asthma, pregnancy. [NHS]
- **Loperamide** 4 mg first, then 2 mg after each loose stool; self-treatment max 12 mg/24 h and no more than 2 days; overdose causes dangerous heart rhythms; not with fever or blood in stool. [NHS]
- **Hyoscine butylbromide** 10–20 mg up to 4×/day for cramps (not with glaucoma or urinary retention). [NHS]
- **Antihistamines:** non-sedating cetirizine 10 mg for runny nose; sedating ones only occasionally for sleep, next-day drowsiness, never with alcohol or other sedatives. Avoid cough syrups altogether.
- **Doctor-only:** buprenorphine, methadone, clonidine, lofexidine, ondansetron/antiemetics, short sleep aids. [NICE CG52; MedlinePlus]

## 7. Don't
- Don't take leftover syrup or any other opioid (tolerance drops fast → overdose risk; most opioid overdose deaths follow a recent detox). [MedlinePlus]
- No alcohol, benzodiazepines (e.g. alprazolam) or sleeping pills without a doctor (breathing suppression).
- Don't exceed loperamide limits; don't drive when exhausted or drowsy.

## 8. Red flags → doctor / nearest hospital
Can't keep fluids down >24 h; signs of dehydration (very little dark urine, dizziness/fainting); blood in vomit or stool; chest pain or very fast heartbeat; seizure; confusion; high fever; slow/shallow breathing or unresponsiveness after any use; thoughts of self-harm → SOS / trusted contact / hospital now.

## 9. PAWS
Post-acute/protracted withdrawal: mood swings, anxiety, anhedonia, poor sleep, fatigue, poor concentration, cravings, in waves for weeks to months; improves with time, sleep routine, exercise, structure and counselling. [SAMHSA Advisory 2010; SAMHSA TIP 63]
